**Subject: The land they called bush**

In 2019, we bought land in a place people called the bush.

They were not wrong about what it looked like. There was nothing there — no estates around it, no traffic, nothing that photographed well. What they were wrong about was what they were looking at. They were pricing the land's appearance. We were pricing its trajectory.

Today that land is surrounded by development and worth multiples of what was paid. That is not luck. That is what time does to a well-positioned asset.

**The one idea this week: value lives in trajectory, not in present appearance.**

The best investment locations usually look boring before they become obvious. By the time a location is obvious, its price already includes the obviousness. The early buyer paid pre-announcement price for the same land the late buyer pays a premium for after the announcement.

So how do you see trajectory before it becomes appearance? You read infrastructure the way other people read news. Everywhere the Lagos State Government has placed a road, a rail line, or a logistics hub, land values have followed. The Lagos Mass Rail Transit. The Epe Agric Hub. These are not news stories. They are investment signals.

**Do this:**

1. Pick one corridor you have been curious about and find the actual government infrastructure plan for it — not a flyer's summary of it.
2. Ask where the city is going, not where it currently is. Sentiment is not a strategy; you buy where the city is going, not where you grew up.
3. Before any money moves, run the full verification: title documents, C of O, Governor's Consent, survey plan, chain of title. Trajectory without documentation is just a well-researched mistake.

**One number worth knowing:** over 500,000 land scam incidents happen across Lagos and Ogun State every year — which is why point 3 is not optional, no matter how good the corridor looks.

The question to sit with this week: am I preparing for opportunities, or am I positioning for them?
