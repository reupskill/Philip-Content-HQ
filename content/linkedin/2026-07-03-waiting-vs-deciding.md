Over 1,000 clients in five years have taught me the difference between waiting to invest and deciding to invest.

It is not income. I have watched people earning very well wait for years. I have watched people earning modestly buy their first plot at 24.

The difference is what they believe the money is for.

The waiter believes investing is a destination — something you arrive at after you become comfortable. The decider understands that investing is the vehicle. You do not accumulate money and then buy land. You buy land and let the land accumulate value while you continue to grow.

At our Uvest Metro allocation, over 80% of the buyers were between 20 and 29 years old.

These were not wealthy people. They were disciplined people. That is a different thing entirely.

They redirected money from things that depreciate — phones, outfits, lifestyle upgrades — toward something that appreciates. The math doesn't lie; the mindset does.

Meanwhile, the person waiting until they feel completely ready is always buying at the price someone else set years ago.

The market rewards the disciplined, not the occasionally motivated.

So here is the question I would ask you to sit with — not answer in the comments, just sit with:

What decision are you delaying today that you will pay double for tomorrow?

That is not a rhetorical question. It is a calculation.
