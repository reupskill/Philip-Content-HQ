# A C of O Alone Is Not Enough

*The five documents every Nigerian buyer must verify before committing any funds.*

A few years ago, a young man sat across from one of our consultants and asked the question almost every first-time buyer asks in some form: "But it has a C of O — so it's safe, right?"

He had already been shown land by another company. The Certificate of Occupancy existed. It was real. What he did not know was that a real document and a clean transaction are not the same thing. A C of O can be genuine and the person selling to you can still have no right to sell. A C of O can be genuine and the land can still be under acquisition, in dispute, or already sold to someone else.

He was lucky. He asked before he paid. Many people ask after.

## The fear is rational. Treat it that way.

Over 500,000 land scam incidents happen across Lagos and Ogun State every year. Over ₦100 billion is estimated to be lost to fraudulent property transactions annually. When someone tells me they are afraid of buying land in Nigeria, I never argue with them. The hesitation is not irrational. It is rational.

But rational fear has a rational answer, and it is not "avoid real estate forever." It is verification. The buyer who has been taught how to verify does not need to be brave. They need to be thorough.

Here is the framework.

## 1. The Certificate of Occupancy — what it is and what it is not

The C of O is the government's formal recognition of your right to occupy and use land, typically for 99 years. You must confirm it exists, and you must confirm it is genuine — which means verifying it at the state land registry, not admiring the paper.

What it is not: proof that the person in front of you owns the land, proof the land is free of dispute, or proof it is outside government acquisition. A C of O tells you the land has a recognized root. It does not tell you the story since then. That story lives in the next four checks.

## 2. Governor's Consent — what it actually protects

When land with a C of O changes hands, the transfer legally requires the Governor's Consent. This is the step many transactions skip, and it is the step that protects *you* specifically — it is what makes the transfer to you recognized, not just the original allocation.

If you are buying from someone who bought from someone, ask to see the consent on the previous transfers. A chain with missing consents is a chain with weak links, and you are proposing to become the last link.

## 3. The survey plan — where the land actually is

The survey plan tells you the precise coordinates and boundaries of what you are buying. Two things to do with it: chart it at the Surveyor General's office to confirm the land is not under government acquisition or committed to another scheme, and physically match it to the land you were shown. Buyers have paid for coordinates that were not the plot they walked on.

## 4. The chain of title — the full history

Who has owned this land, in what sequence, with what documentation at each step? Every gap in that chain is a question someone may one day answer in court at your expense. Ask for the history. A credible seller has it organized. A seller who becomes impatient when you ask for history is answering a different question — about themselves.

## 5. The seller's track record — the document nobody prints

The most important question to ask any developer is not "How much?" It is "What have you already delivered, and can I verify it?"

Can they show you land they promised and delivered on time, documented, with clients who can speak to the experience? Can you visit completed estates? Can you speak to existing landowners independently — not the ones seated beside the salesperson?

Anyone can sell. Not everyone can deliver. At Uvest we have delivered thirteen estates, and I tell prospects to verify us the same way I am telling you to verify anyone: independently. A company that has genuinely delivered will never be offended by that request. A company that is offended by it has told you what you needed to know.

## The deeper point

Notice what this framework does to fear. It converts it from a feeling into a checklist. The buyer who has been taught how to think about land, how to verify a title, and how to evaluate a location is a different buyer — more confident, less fearful, more decisive.

That is why education sits at the center of everything we do at Uvest. Education creates trust. And in this market, trust is not a soft word. It is the infrastructure the entire transaction stands on.

## Before you go

Ask yourself: what decision am I delaying today that I will pay double for tomorrow? If the honest answer is "buying land, because I'm afraid," you now have the beginning of the answer to the fear.

Start with one piece of verified information about a location you have been curious about. Learn to read a C of O. Learn what Governor's Consent means. Then take the first step.

The first decision does not have to be perfect. It has to be made.
